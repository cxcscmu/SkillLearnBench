# Seven-Character Regulated Verse (七言律诗)

A guide for composing traditional Chinese poetry in the seven-character regulated verse format.

## Core Structure
- **Length**: Exactly 8 lines.
- **Line Length**: Exactly 7 characters per line.
- **Couplets**: The poem consists of 4 couplets:
  1. **Shoulian (首联)**: Lines 1-2 (Head couplet) - Introduces the theme.
  2. **Hanlian (颔联)**: Lines 3-4 (Jaw couplet) - Develops the theme; MUST be parallel (antithesis).
  3. **Jinglian (颈联)**: Lines 5-6 (Neck couplet) - Transitions or deepens the theme; MUST be parallel (antithesis).
  4. **Wei-lian (尾联)**: Lines 7-8 (Tail couplet) - Concludes the poem.

## Rhyming (Modern Mandarin)
- Rhyme typically occurs at the end of even-numbered lines (2, 4, 6, 8).
- Line 1 may also rhyme (optional but common).
- Use modern Mandarin pronunciation for rhyming.
- All rhyming lines should share the same vowel sound (and often the same tone, usually level tone/平声).

## Parallelism (Dui-zhang 对仗)
- Required for the middle two couplets (lines 3-4 and 5-6).
- Nouns should match nouns, verbs match verbs, adjectives match adjectives.
- Imagery should be balanced (e.g., "mountain" vs "river", "old" vs "new").

## Tone Patterns (Ping-ze 平仄)
- Traditionally, regulated verse follows strict tonal patterns (Level/Ping and Oblique/Ze).
- While modern Mandarin tones (1, 2 = Level; 3, 4 = Oblique) can be used, prioritize the semantic flow and structural parallelism first unless strict classical adherence is requested.

## Common Themes
- Nature, parting, history, war, and personal reflection.
- Use of classical imagery (e.g., autumn wind, lonely moon, frontier fortress).
