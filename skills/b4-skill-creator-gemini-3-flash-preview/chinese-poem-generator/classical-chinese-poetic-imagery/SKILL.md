# Classical Chinese Poetic Imagery (War & Peace)

A collection of common imagery and themes for expressing the suffering of war and the longing for peace.

## War-Related Imagery
- **Frontier (塞外, 边疆)**: Harsh environments, desolate sands, cold winds (胡风).
- **Weapons (干戈, 铠甲, 宝剑)**: Shields and axes (symbols of war), armor, swords.
- **Signs of Combat (烽火, 硝烟)**: Beacon fires (signaling invasion), smoke from cannons or fires.
- **Human Cost (白骨, 枯骸, 孤儿)**: White bones in the grass, orphans, mourning widows.
- **Desolation (荒村, 废垒)**: Abandoned villages, crumbling fortresses.

## Peace-Related Imagery
- **Homestead (故里, 田园)**: Returning home, peaceful gardens and fields.
- **Farming (锄犁, 桑麻)**: Plows and hoes, mulberry and hemp (symbols of rural life).
- **Daily Life (炊烟, 渔樵)**: Cooking smoke (sign of life), fishermen and woodcutters.
- **Nature (春风, 归燕)**: Spring breeze, returning swallows (sign of stability and return).
- **Music (短笛, 琴声)**: Short flutes, zither sounds (contrasting with war drums).

## Expressing Longing (思念/寄愿)
- **Dreaming of Home (梦还乡)**: Seeing family in dreams.
- **Letters (家书, 雁书)**: Families waiting for letters from the front (烽火连三月，家书抵万金).
- **Ancient Examples (周公, 尧舜)**: Evoking legendary eras of great peace and prosperity.

## Stylistic Considerations
- **Contrast**: Juxtapose the harshness of the battlefield with the gentleness of home life.
- **Metonymy**: Use "beacon fires" (烽火) to mean war, "plow and hoe" (锄犁) to mean peace.
- **Emotional Arc**: Start with external desolation and move toward internal longing or a vision of the future.
