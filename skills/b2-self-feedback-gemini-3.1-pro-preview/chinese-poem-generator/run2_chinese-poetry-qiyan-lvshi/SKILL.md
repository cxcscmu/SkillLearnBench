# Advanced Seven-character Regulated Verse (七言律诗) Guide

A seven-character regulated verse (七言律诗) is a highly structured form of classical Chinese poetry. It consists of exactly 8 lines, 7 characters per line.

## Tonal Rules (Pingze) based on Modern Mandarin
*   **Level Tone (平, Ping):** Modern Mandarin Tones 1 and 2 (e.g., mā, má).
*   **Oblique Tone (仄, Ze):** Modern Mandarin Tones 3 and 4 (e.g., mǎ, mà).

### Standard Metrical Patterns
The tonal structure of a poem is determined by its first line. There are four basic sentence patterns:
1.  **平起平收 (Ping start, Ping end):** 平平仄仄仄平平
2.  **仄起平收 (Ze start, Ping end):** 仄仄平平仄仄平
3.  **平起仄收 (Ping start, Ze end):** 平平仄仄平平仄
4.  **仄起仄收 (Ze start, Ze end):** 仄仄平平平仄仄

A typical "Ze start, rhyming first line" (仄起入韵) pattern is:
*   Line 1: 仄仄平平仄仄平 (Rhyme)
*   Line 2: 平平仄仄仄平平 (Rhyme)
*   Line 3: 平平仄仄平平仄
*   Line 4: 仄仄平平仄仄平 (Rhyme)
*   Line 5: 仄仄平平平仄仄
*   Line 6: 平平仄仄仄平平 (Rhyme)
*   Line 7: 平平仄仄平平仄
*   Line 8: 仄仄平平仄仄平 (Rhyme)

### Advanced Strictness Rules
*   **一三五不论，二四六分明:** The 1st, 3rd, and 5th characters of a line are generally flexible. The 2nd, 4th, and 6th must strictly follow the pattern.
*   **Avoid 孤平 (Lone Ping):** In lines ending in Ping (平平仄仄仄平平 or 仄仄平平仄仄平), there must not be only a single Ping tone (excluding the final rhyme character). For instance, in `平平仄仄仄平平`, if the 3rd character is changed to Ze, it becomes `平平仄仄仄平平` which is fine. But in `仄仄平平仄仄平`, changing the 3rd character to Ze creates `仄仄仄平仄仄平`, leaving only one Ping before the rhyme—this is forbidden.
*   **Avoid 三平调 (Three Ping Ending):** The last three characters of a line cannot all be Ping tones. A line meant to end in `仄平平` cannot become `平平平`.

## Rhyme (Yun)
*   In modern Mandarin poetry, rhymes are often based on the **Thirteen Tracks (十三辙)**.
*   Rhyming occurs on the even lines (2, 4, 6, 8) and often line 1.
*   The rhyme must be in the **Ping tone**.
*   All rhyming characters in a poem must belong to the same rhyme category (e.g., "言前辙" covering modern an, ian, uan, üan).

## Antithesis (Duizhang)
*   **Jaw Couplet (Lines 3 & 4):** Must form a strict antithetical pair. (e.g., Noun matches Noun, Verb matches Verb).
*   **Neck Couplet (Lines 5 & 6):** Must form a strict antithetical pair.
*   The semantic meaning should contrast or complement each other.

## Theme & Perspective
When writing about war from an ordinary person's perspective, focus on relatable, grounded imagery: ruined homes, separated families, hunger, cold, and a desperate plea for normalcy (plowshares instead of swords).
