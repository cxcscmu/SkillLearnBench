# Mandarin Rhyming for Classical Poetry

## Modern Mandarin Pronunciation System

### The Four Tones
```
1st Tone (ā): High and level
2nd Tone (á): Rising from middle to high
3rd Tone (ǎ): Falling then rising (dip)
4th Tone (à): Sharp fall from high to low
```

In regulated verse composition:
- Tones determine 平仄 patterns (1st & 2nd = 平, 3rd & 4th = 仄)
- Rhyming words must share the same final vowel sound + consonant pattern
- Tone marks can be different; only the vowel+consonant final must match

## Pinyin Finals (Rhyme Sets)

### Finals Ending in -an
- **an** (安/安): ān, án, ǎn, àn [pīnyīn: bān, pán, etc.]
- **ian**: xiān, miǎn, diàn
- **uan**: duān, guán, etc.
- **üan**: xuān, etc.

Common characters for 安 rhyme:
- 安 (ān) - peace/safety
- 山 (shān) - mountain
- 关 (guān) - gate/close
- 还 (huán) - return
- 间 (jiān) - space/between
- 坚 (jiān) - firm
- 颜 (yán) - face/color
- 年 (nián) - year
- 天 (tiān) - sky/heaven
- 千 (qiān) - thousand

### Finals Ending in -ing
- **ing**: dīng, míng, píng, tíng
- **uing**: guīng, duīng
- **iing**: (not commonly used)

Common characters for -ing rhyme:
- 平 (píng) - flat/peace
- 鸣 (míng) - cry/sound
- 京 (jīng) - capital
- 情 (qíng) - emotion
- 生 (shēng) - life/birth
- 听 (tīng) - listen
- 云 (yún) - cloud
- 明 (míng) - bright

### Finals Ending in -ang
- **ang**: bāng, fāng, gāng, cháng
- **iang**: jiāng, liáng, xiáng
- **uang**: zhuāng, chuáng, guāng

Common characters:
- 堂 (táng) - hall
- 乡 (xiāng) - village/countryside
- 光 (guāng) - light
- 伤 (shāng) - wound/harm
- 长 (cháng) - long
- 江 (jiāng) - river

### Finals Ending in -en
- **en**: bēn, gēn, ōu
- **ien**: diēn, etc.
- **uen**: guēn, kūn
- **ün**: lǜn, jūn

### Finals Ending in -er
- **er**: ér, hěr
- Most standalone rhyme (儿/女/者 style)

## Identifying Rhyme Families

### Method 1: Same Final Sound
Two characters rhyme if they share the same final vowel sound:
- 天 (tiān) + 年 (nián) = both end in "an" sound ✓
- 天 (tiān) + 名 (míng) = different finals ✗

### Method 2: Checking Modern Pronunciation
1. Write character's pinyin with tone marks
2. Remove the tone mark
3. Keep only the final vowel pattern
4. Characters with identical finals rhyme

Example:
- 安 (ān) → final is "an"
- 山 (shān) → final is "an"
- Both rhyme! ✓

The -an rhyme is ideal for peace poetry:

| Character | Pinyin | Meaning | Tone |
|-----------|--------|---------|------|
| 安 | ān | peace, safe | 1st |
| 山 | shān | mountain | 1st |
| 关 | guān | gate, close | 1st |

| 天 | tiān | sky, heaven | 1st |

## Alternative Peace Rhymes

### -ING Rhyme (平/鸣/生/听)
Clean, bright sound - good for hope/peace imagery
- 平, 鸣, 京, 生, 情, 听, 明, 云, 行, 英

### -IANG Rhyme (乡/伤/光/长)
Versatile, evocative - good for village/nature imagery
- 乡, 伤, 光, 长, 江, 香, 狼, 殇, 祥, 张

## Tone Pairing Rules for Rhyming

### For Line-Final Position (Most Important)
The rhyming character should follow the 平仄 requirement:
- If line ends with 平 tone rhyme: characters with 1st or 2nd tone
- If line ends with 仄 tone rhyme: characters with 3rd or 4th tone

For the -an rhyme:
- 1st/2nd tone characters: 安、天、山、关、还、年、闲、颜、难、乡
- 3rd/4th tone characters: 眼、看、汉、乱、管、唤、贬

## Practical Rhyming Workflow

1. **Select rhyme**: Choose -an for peace theme
2. **Pick rhyming characters** for lines 2, 4, 6, 8
3. **Check tones** match 平仄 patterns required
4. **Build lines** with non-rhyming characters (lines 1, 3, 5, 7)
5. **Verify pronunciation** using pinyin finals, not individual character sounds

## Common Rhyming Errors to Avoid

❌ Using characters that "sound similar" but have different finals
- 天 (tiān, final: an) ≠ 真 (zhēn, final: en)
- These don't rhyme!

❌ Mixing tone requirements
- A line ending with 平 tone shouldn't have 仄 tone rhyme character
- Violates the regulated pattern

❌ Using modern colloquial pronunciation instead of standard pinyin
- Always use standard Mandarin pinyin
- Not dialectal variations

## Verification Checklist
- [ ] All 4 rhyming characters share identical pinyin final
- [ ] All rhyming characters have appropriate tones for line positions
- [ ] No repeated rhyming characters in the poem
- [ ] Rhyme sounds are recognizable and clear in modern Mandarin
- [ ] Rhyming characters make semantic sense in context
